Key findings

  • All 4,500 units sold out in 9 months: USD 94,238 revenue (excl. VAT) and USD 33,771 profit before tax (36% margin).
  • The USD 28,777 spent before the first sale (production, design, first shipments) was earned back in January 2026.
  • Tunisia made most of the money: 44% margin and USD 10.75 profit per unit. Norway was only just profitable (7% margin, USD 1.01 per unit): lower price (200 NOK), discounts to compete and expensive ads and delivery.
  • Best channel: Tunisia online at USD 14.27 per unit. Parapharmacies paid less per unit but needed no advertising and bought in bulk.
  • The landed cost of a unit in Tunisia rose from USD 7.65 (first shipment) to USD 9.69 (last), +27%, driven by freight and the duty charged on it.
  • A second run with freight 25% above the last shipment and ads +15% would make USD 30,279 instead of USD 38,821 before brand costs (-22%), with USD 20,000+ paid up front again, so the project was stopped.

Break-even freight per unit: Tunisia online: $12.15; Tunisia parapharmacy: $8.15; Norway online: $3.91.

Decision: stop after the first run

  • Tunisia was clearly profitable, especially online, but every shipment cost more to land.
  • Norway stayed only just profitable at 200 NOK: with strong competition, freight, delivery and ads took almost the whole price. Higher fees would push it to break-even.
  • A second run needs USD 20,000+ up front again, for clearly less profit.
  • What could have changed the result: sea freight in one bigger shipment, focus on Tunisia (online + parapharmacies) and leave Norway, or raise the Norway price with a smaller range.
